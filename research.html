Creating mathematics for biology

Mathematics built for the complexity of living systems

Biology doesn't fit neatly into the mathematics built for physics or engineering. Living systems are nonlinear, high-dimensional, and irreducibly messy. Their data lives on curved surfaces, in tensor structures, in topological shapes, not flat Euclidean space. Here are the tools we use.

Applied Algebraic Geometry & Tensors

Algebraic geometry works in the higher-dimensional spaces where the real structure lives — and to know, with mathematical certainty, when that structure can be uniquely recovered.

  • Details

Topological Data Analysis

Shape is information. Topology detects and measures geometric structure in biological data — across scales, across noise — features that alone algebra and statistics can't see.

  • Details

Software & Computational Mathematics

This mathematics requires new algorithms. We build computational tools that make theoretical methods usable — by other researchers, in other systems, at scale.

  • Details

Bridging precision and complexity

Where algebraic precision meets biological uncertainty

Biological data is not clean. It's incomplete, noisy, high-dimensional, and often shaped by uncertain knowledge. The mathematics in this layer operates in that reality — combining the rigour of algebra and geometry with the messiness of real data, the uncertainty of statistical inference, and the structures encoded in biological networks.

Identifiability, Statistics & AI

What can a model predict? A better question: Can its parameters ever be uniquely determined from data at all? If the answer is no, no amount of computation will produce a trustworthy result.

  • Details

Dynamical Systems & Mathematical Modelling

Living systems grow, evolve, oscillate, bifurcate, and self-organise, they aren't static — and understanding why requires mathematics that can describe change, stability, and sudden transitions between them.

  • Details

Biological Networks

Every cell is governed by networks — of genes, proteins, signals, and feedback loops. This prior knowledge about how a system is wired is too valuable to ignore, and too complex to reason about without algebra and combinatorics.

  • Details

Morphology & Spatial Biology

Geometric and topological methods show spatial organisation in tissues and imaging data that no standard analysis could find — including cell types and structures that were previously invisible. Because where a cell is matters as much as what it is.

  • Details

Translational applications

Answering biomedical questions that could not be answered otherwise

The word translational has a precise meaning in biomedical science: research that moves between mathematical or experimental insight and clinical or therapeutic application. The group's work is translational in exactly this sense.

Omics

A single modern experiment can describe the activity of every gene, every protein, in a tissue sample. The challenge is not producing this data. It is finding the mathematical framework that can reveal what it actually means.

  • Details

Oncology

Cancer does not follow a single script. Tumour cells rewire their signalling, that vary between patients, between cell populations, and over time — and understanding that variation requires mathematics to interpretable structure in high-dimensional, heterogeneous data.

  • Details

Immunology

The immune system is one of the most complex dynamical systems in biology — capable of extraordinary precision and catastrophic dysregulation, often governed by differences too subtle for any single experiment to isolate. Mathematical modelling makes it possible to ask questions that experiment alone cannot reach.

  • Details

Neuroscience

Neural circuits encode information in geometry as much as in activity. Topological and network methods developed by the group reveal hidden structure in how the brain represents the world — structure that only becomes visible when you have the mathematics to look for it.
